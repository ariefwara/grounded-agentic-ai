# Title

The Right Level of Trust for Every Conversation

# Central Promise

Customers can explore public information without unnecessary friction, while personal information and sensitive actions receive identification or verification appropriate to the situation.

# Narrative Arc

1. Many service experiences make every visitor log in before they can ask a simple question.
2. Other experiences expose personal service too easily.
3. The assistant distinguishes between open discovery, a known customer context, and a request that needs stronger verification.
4. A prospective property customer may compare homes and arrange a tour with minimal information.
5. A pharmacy, insurance, or banking customer may need to establish identity and answer business-appropriate verification questions before protected information or actions continue.
6. Relevant customer context can remain available during the conversation so the same information is not requested repeatedly.
7. The level and method of verification vary by business and sensitivity rather than following one universal script.

# Customer Benefit

Easy access when the request is low risk and appropriate protection when the request becomes personal.

# Business Benefit

The business can offer convenient conversational service without treating every interaction as equally sensitive.

# Important Boundary

Do not present this as a replacement for all production authentication, authorization, privacy, or compliance controls.

Do not claim that the feature secures data, fulfils obligations, or determines regulatory compliance. Do not invent exact identifiers, verification questions, or booking details.
