# Title

Recommendations Customers Can Believe In

# Central Promise

The assistant helps customers compare realistic choices, understand trade-offs, and become confident enough to decide without relying on a generic sales pitch.

# Narrative Arc

1. Showing a small list is not the same as helping someone choose.
2. The assistant first discovers priorities such as fit, price, timing, availability, specifications, or service expectations.
3. It compares viable alternatives using relevant business facts.
4. It explains why an option fits and where another option may be stronger.
5. It detects the concern blocking a decision and addresses it with specifications, availability, warranties, service terms, or applicable policies.
6. A policy can protect the business while also reducing customer risk. Its customer benefit becomes a fair reason to proceed, with conditions kept clear.
7. The recommendation arrives after sufficient context, not immediately after the first message.

# Example Direction

Use the retail air fryer journey as the primary example: the customer cannot inspect the product before pickup, compares several suitable choices, remains uncertain, and becomes more comfortable after receiving factual product details and a clear explanation of the applicable exchange protection.

Briefly contrast this with a beauty consultation or travel choice to show that decision support changes by domain.

# Customer Benefit

A decision that feels informed, personal, and lower risk.

# Business Benefit

Product knowledge and customer-friendly policies become useful commercial strengths while the conversation remains transparent.

# Important Boundary

Persuasion must be factual and fair. Do not invent urgency, hide conditions, guarantee satisfaction, or pressure the customer.

Do not invent product specifications, policy conditions, treatment effects, or recommendation wording. Describe comparison and objection handling conceptually.
