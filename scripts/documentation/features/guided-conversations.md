# Title

Conversations That Lead Customers Forward

# Central Promise

Customers can begin with a few uncertain words. The assistant takes responsibility for guiding the conversation toward a clear need and useful next step.

# Narrative Arc

1. Traditional customer service expects people to know the right product, department, terminology, or form.
2. Real customers are busy and type short messages without complete context.
3. The assistant introduces what it can help with and asks one focused question at a time.
4. It retains relevant answers, avoids needless repetition, and keeps the interaction moving.
5. It can support corrections, changed preferences, and a customer who is still unsure.
6. Different domains guide differently: retail discovers preferences, beauty begins with the concern, property explores location and budget, and delivery gathers the details needed to understand the issue.

# Customer Benefit

Less effort, less confusion, and a conversation that feels like competent service rather than a search box.

# Business Benefit

Better-formed requests, more customers reaching a meaningful next step, and a consistent service experience without forcing everyone through one rigid script.

# Important Boundary

Do not imply that the assistant can answer or resolve every request. Emphasize purposeful guidance within the configured business journey.

Keep the article focused on guiding the conversation. Do not make completion of an action the main story, and do not invent a new customer example outside the examples already listed.
