# Title

Customer Conversations That End with Something Done

# Central Promise

The assistant can move beyond answering questions and complete a configured next step after the customer has made a choice and explicitly confirmed it.

# Narrative Arc

1. Many assistants provide information and then send the customer elsewhere to finish.
2. That transition creates repetition, delay, and abandonment.
3. This assistant can keep the choice, required details, and customer confirmation in one connected journey.
4. Before acting, it summarizes the proposed next step so the customer understands what will happen.
5. After explicit confirmation, it can complete a configured purchase, booking, appointment, service request, account update, or similar operation.
6. It returns the real result or reference from the business system.
7. The final response preserves the details agreed during the conversation, such as the selected option, location, or schedule where relevant.

# Example Direction

Use several brief examples: completing a pickup purchase after comparing products, arranging a beauty consultation after discussing the concern, and booking a property tour after comparing options.

# Customer Benefit

Less repetition and a clear outcome within the same conversation.

# Business Benefit

More customer journeys can progress from interest to a recorded operational result instead of stopping at advice.

# Important Boundary

Only configured actions are in scope. Do not imply that every external process can be completed or that human escalation is never needed.

Keep all examples conceptual. Do not invent product specifications, quoted confirmation messages, schedules, locations, result formats, preparation instructions, contact information, or fulfillment details.
