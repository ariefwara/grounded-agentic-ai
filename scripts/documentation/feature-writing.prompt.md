# Writing Task

Write one public-facing Markdown article about a single product feature using the supplied feature outline.

The article must make a business owner or product team interested in trying the system. Describe what the feature feels like for customers, why it matters to the business, and what kind of outcome it creates. Do not explain the internal implementation. Deeper mechanics belong in the separate Solution documentation.

# Product Context

The product is an AI customer service assistant that guides people from short, incomplete messages toward confident decisions and completed next steps.

Customers should not need to understand a company's workflows. They may begin with messages such as "I need an air fryer," "my delivery is late," or "I want something for my skin." The assistant can introduce how it helps, ask focused questions, retain relevant conversation context, compare realistic options, address uncertainty with business facts, and move toward an appropriate outcome.

Different businesses require different journeys. A retail assistant may help compare products and complete a pickup purchase. A beauty clinic assistant may understand a concern, discuss suitable treatments, and arrange a consultation. A property assistant may help a prospective customer compare options and book a tour. Travel, delivery, subscription, pharmacy, insurance, and banking conversations may require different questions, levels of trust, and outcomes.

Persuasion must remain factual and fair. Product facts, availability, warranties, service terms, and applicable policies may help customers decide. Policies that protect the business can also reduce customer risk and become a legitimate reason to proceed, while all important conditions remain clear.

Public and pre-sales conversations should remain easy. Personal or sensitive requests may require identification or verification appropriate to the business and situation.

After helping the customer choose, the assistant may summarize the next step, obtain explicit confirmation, complete a configured purchase, booking, appointment, service request, account update, or similar action, and return the real result or reference.

Each deployment may have its own identity, offers, tone, guidance style, customer journey, policies, and visual appearance.

# Article Direction

- Use the exact H1 title supplied in the feature outline.
- Open with a strong, concise value proposition.
- Focus deeply on this feature only.
- Explain the customer problem before describing the better experience.
- Show how the experience unfolds through realistic examples.
- Connect the customer benefit to a concrete business benefit.
- Use clear, confident English and short, readable sections.
- Write between 900 and 1,200 words. Do not exceed 1,200 words.
- Make the article persuasive without sounding exaggerated.
- End with an invitation to try the feature in a complete scenario.
- Mention that the Solution section explains the deeper flow where useful.
- Return only the final Markdown document.

# Factual Boundaries

- Do not include file names, configuration formats, programming languages, frameworks, databases, APIs, model providers, containers, or deployment instructions.
- Do not include internal gate names, evaluator names, tracing, caching, tokens, routing, prompts, or architectural participants.
- Do not invent quantities, performance claims, customer results, policy conditions, resolution options, or result formats.
- Do not claim that the assistant remembers everything, eliminates every handoff, or supports every business action.
- Do not claim universal production readiness, compliance, accuracy, or automation.
- Use only examples and capabilities present in the product context and feature outline.
- Describe deployments as configured for each business, not separately built products.
- Do not invent product names, specifications, prices, percentages, time periods, dates, locations, people, schedules, policy windows, policy requirements, treatment effects, medical guidance, payment steps, fulfillment promises, emails, forms, or contact channels.
- Do not write fabricated assistant dialogue containing details that were not supplied.
- Do not promise improvements to conversion, engagement, abandonment, returns, speed, satisfaction, compliance, or revenue. Explain the intended business value without presenting it as a measured result.
- Avoid absolute words such as "always," "never," "nothing is lost," "every customer," and "the assistant already knows."
- Do not say that a sensitive business has met its obligations merely because this feature is used.
- Do not place invented assistant or customer messages in quotation marks or blockquotes.
- Keep examples conceptual. Describe the progression of the conversation without supplying exact products, specifications, policies, questions, answers, schedules, locations, or confirmations.
- Do not say that the feature itself protects, secures, or keeps information private. Say that it supports an appropriately controlled customer journey.
- Before returning the article, silently remove any unsupported detail, absolute claim, fabricated dialogue, or measurable business outcome.
